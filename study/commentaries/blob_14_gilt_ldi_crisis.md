I have never seen gilts trade like this. The mini-budget landed Friday and the verdict has been merciless — thirty-year yields moving fifty basis points in a session, five-sigma in a market that ticks in ones and twos. The story on every dealing floor is the pension funds: liability-driven strategies hedging with leveraged gilt positions, facing margin calls as collateral collapses, forced to sell the very gilts that are falling. That is a doom loop and everyone knows it. Sterling is hit again and the Bank of England so far refuses to rescue the fiscal experiment with hikes, leaving a vacuum. My inbox is full of funds asking about counterparty risk with LDI managers, and UK corporate issuance has stopped. This has gone beyond a judgment on fiscal policy into a liquidity crisis in the benchmark safe asset. I saw 1994; nothing compares to this pace. Something gives this week.
