The canal is the bottleneck that keeps getting worse. Drought has pulled Gatun Lake down so far that daily transits are being cut toward the low twenties, drafts are restricted, and waiting times run for days; shipowners are paying record auction fees, into the millions of dollars, just to jump the queue. Now the Red Sea is compounding it: carriers are suspending Suez sailings after attacks on boxships and rerouting around the Cape of Good Hope, adding ten days or more each way and absorbing effective capacity nobody budgeted for. Spot rates are repricing violently, with Asia to Europe up by half or more in a matter of days, and alliances are redrawing schedules monthly. My working assumption is expensive freight and stretching delivery times well into next year, because there is no spare tonnage to fix any of this quickly.
