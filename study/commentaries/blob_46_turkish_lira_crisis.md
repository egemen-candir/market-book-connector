The lira is in freefall, knocking on seven to the dollar before wobbling back through six and a half. This is no longer a rates story; it is a diplomatic crisis colliding with an economy starved of hard currency. Washington's tariffs and the Brunson standoff show no sign of easing, and every headline out of Ankara seems engineered to scare dollars out. The president insists higher interest rates cause inflation and begs citizens to hand over the gold and dollars under the pillow, while the central bank's midnight package, looser reserve requirements plus a late-liquidity hike that has yet to touch market rates, convinces nobody that it can act independently. Corporate foreign-currency debt is the real time bomb: bank CDS are blowing out, eurobonds are collapsing and wholesale funding is shut. I am treating anything Turkish as uninvestable until genuine tightening arrives.
