Yesterday May WTI settled at minus $37 a barrel — negative, a first in history — and the equity market barely flinched. The S&P has rallied hard off the March lows, the Nasdaq has clawed back to positive for the year, and the tapes tell two different stories. The oil collapse is largely plumbing: the May contract expired with Cushing storage nearly full, longs had nowhere to roll in a vicious contango, and index-ETF mechanics amplified the squeeze. June crude trades back in the twenties, a wide premium that says the dislocation was expiry arithmetic as much as demand. Meanwhile stimulus checks are landing, reopening chatter grows louder by the day, and equities are pricing the other side of the shutdown, storage tanks notwithstanding.
