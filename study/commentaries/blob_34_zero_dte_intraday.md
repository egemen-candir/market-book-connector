Something structural has changed in how this market trades, and I keep coming back to the zero-day options. They are roughly half of SPX option volume now, and the intraday tape has acquired a mechanical quality I have not seen in twenty years. Mornings drift; then dealer hedging takes over and price accelerates toward the largest strike concentration, often snapping to round numbers before the close, then settling the moment expiry passes. Directionless most days, yet when a data print lands the move compresses into an hour of intensity because everyone trades the same expiry. Volatility sellers love it — realized vol stays low and the premium income prints — but risk is concentrating somewhere nobody stress-tested: a gap event with millions of contracts expiring that afternoon could force hedging one way with no natural buyers. The close-to-close tape looks calm, but the plumbing underneath is more leveraged than ever.
