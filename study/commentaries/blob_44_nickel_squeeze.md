Nickel has broken the LME. The metal nearly doubled today, trading above $100,000 a tonne after starting the week near $30,000, on a violent short squeeze, with desks tracing the pain to a giant position tied to Tsingshan's chairman and the market on edge over war-disrupted Russian supply. The exchange suspended nickel trading this morning and, remarkably, is canceling the trades executed during the spike, a decision that has floor desks howling about the sanctity of mark-to-market. Brokers are staring at margin calls they cannot collect, with some clients simply blown up; the day's moves cannot be settled. Copper, aluminum and zinc are bid alongside as the whole complex prices Russian supply risk. Where nickel prints when the market reopens is anyone's guess: half the street is a forced buyer and the other half cannot finance positions at these levels.
