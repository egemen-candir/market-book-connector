The ten-year is crossing five percent this morning for the first time since 2007, and the tape doesn't like it. Equities opened soft and keep sagging — the S&P is struggling to hold 4,200 and long-duration tech is underperforming as real yields back up. What strikes me is the absence of dip-buying: every Treasury auction finds sellers, term premium is rebuilding, and the higher-for-longer narrative keeps getting reinforced by resilient growth data and firm energy prices. A five percent risk-free rate competes with everything, and I can feel allocators drifting toward money markets. Managers are talking capitulation, not opportunity, and the Middle East flare-up has only added a bid to oil and safe havens. I have no strong view on whether this is a bond-vigilante overshoot or the start of something worse. I just know every valuation model on the street was built for a different rate.
