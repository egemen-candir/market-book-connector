The disconnect is the whole story. We're sitting at ten percent unemployment, GDP cratered, whole sectors of the economy still shut — and the Nasdaq just keeps making new highs, with the S&P having finally cleared its February peak a few days ago. Apple is up something like sixty percent this year and just announced a four-for-one split; Tesla is splitting too, and every announcement lights another fuse under the retail crowd. Call-option volume is printing records — week after week — with tiny premiums chasing the megacaps on Robinhood, and dealers chasing gamma have turned the whole tape self-reinforcing. Jackson Hole brought the Fed's average-inflation framework, which reads as rates pinned near zero for years. Breadth is troubling: five or six names carry the indexes while cyclicals, banks, and value limp along. It feels like a market narrowing into euphoria, and I can't find the bear case that matters.
