Cocoa has gone vertical. New York futures are through ten thousand dollars a tonne, roughly triple where the market traded in the autumn, and every session seems to print a fresh record, obliterating a 1977 high that had stood for nearly five decades. The West African harvest is the whole story: El Niño heat and drought, black pod and swollen shoot disease, and port arrivals in Côte d'Ivoire down almost a third year on year, in a belt that grows two-thirds of the world's beans. Deficit forecasts keep getting worse, grinders are paying up for nearby beans and London sits in a steep backwardation. Exchange margins have been hiked again and again, yet funds keep pressing. Everyone knows parabolas end badly, but with physical beans simply not there I would not stand in front of this.
