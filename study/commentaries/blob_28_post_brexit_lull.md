Mid-August and the streets are empty, the trading desks emptier. Six weeks on from the Brexit vote and the panic has evaporated entirely: the S&P sits at fresh record highs, the VIX spent yesterday down near eleven, and volumes are so thin that a modest institutional order can move a stock. The strangest part is the bond market. Thirty-year Treasury yields have fallen to all-time lows around 2.2%, ten-year paper yields barely 1.5%, and half the European sovereign curve trades in negative territory. Money is being pushed into equities not out of conviction but out of despair over fixed income. The Bank of England's rate cut and fresh stimulus seem to have settled nerves completely. I keep waiting for a proper correction that never arrives. This eerily calm summer unsettles me precisely because nobody else seems bothered.
