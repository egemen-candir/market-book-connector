European gas has gone vertical. Dutch TTF front-month traded through €300/MWh this week and touched roughly €340, more than ten times the pre-crisis norm and a level nobody modeled. Gazprom has cut Nord Stream 1 flows to about twenty percent of capacity, citing turbine excuses few in Berlin believe, and every hiccup in the pipeline now trades like a full shutdown. Utilities and industrials are bidding for cargoes that may not exist; American LNG is crossing the Atlantic at record premiums as Asia steps aside. Germany is finalizing a levy on consumers to rescue gas importers while bracing for rationing, and governments across the EU are drafting emergency measures — price caps, windfall taxes, demand mandates. Storage is filling toward targets, but only because heavy industry has quietly curtailed output. This is an energy economy repricing in real time, and the winter decisions are being made now, in August.
