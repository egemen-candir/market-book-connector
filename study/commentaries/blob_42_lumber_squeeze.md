Lumber is the wildest trade in commodities right now. Futures printed a record above $1,700 per thousand board feet this week, roughly four times where they sat a year ago, and sawmills cannot ship fast enough. North American production is running full tilt after last year's curtailments, but it isn't close to enough: the pandemic DIY renovation boom, near-record housing starts, and dealers restocking like mad have emptied the pipeline. Freight and labor shortages at the mills add friction, and Canada's beetle-killed timber hangs over longer-term supply. Homebuilders are swallowing margin as framing packages add thirty thousand dollars or more to a typical new home, and NAHB keeps screaming about it. The scary part is that nobody I talk to sees inventories rebuilding before summer, and anyone short this market has been steamrolled. Mean reversion keeps getting predicted and keeps not showing up.
