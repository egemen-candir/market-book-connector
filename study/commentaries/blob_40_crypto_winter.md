Bitcoin sits near $4,000, down more than eighty percent from December's peak, and there is no bid anywhere. The ICO market that was the story a year ago has simply evaporated: issuance has dried to a trickle, and most of last year's token sales trade down ninety percent or more from their raises, with founders quietly returning funds or fighting regulators. Ethereum — the rail nearly every ICO was launched on — is back under $110, and the projects that promised to build on it are running out of runway as their treasuries melt. Exchanges are cutting staff, miners are capitulating as margins go negative, and the fork wars in November only deepened the cynicism. For the first time since the mania began, nobody I talk to is buying the dip. That quiet, more than any price, is the story.
