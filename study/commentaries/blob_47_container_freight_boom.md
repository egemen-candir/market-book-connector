Spot rates across the major east-west trades are close to ten times pre-pandemic levels, and even that understates the panic: five figures for a forty-foot box out of Asia, whatever the destination. Off Los Angeles and Long Beach the queue has smashed every record, more than seventy vessels at anchor, and congestion inland means boxes never seem to come back. Every link of the chain is broken at once, from terminal labor to chassis and yard space, with equipment stranded wherever the pandemic left it. Carriers are blanking nothing; sailings are full weeks out and Golden Week brings no relief because the capacity simply is not there. Next year's contract talks are starting and shippers are capitulating to almost any number to secure space. The lines are printing record profits and nothing on the horizon unblocks this.
