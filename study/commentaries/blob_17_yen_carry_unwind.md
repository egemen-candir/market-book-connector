What started as a yen story is now everyone's story. After the Bank of Japan's surprise hike, the yen ripped toward the mid-140s and the carry trade is being unwound with a vengeance — this morning the Nikkei fell more than twelve percent, its worst day since 1987, and the selling hasn't stopped at Japan's shores. The VIX spiked above sixty at the open, European equity futures are gapping lower, and our desks describe liquidity that simply disappears at the touch. Nobody knows how much leveraged yen funding is still embedded in positions around the world, and that uncertainty is itself the trade: forced sellers are meeting thin bids and correlations are going to one. I've seen this shape before, and it ends when either a large official buyer steps in or the marginal seller is exhausted. Until one of those happens, I'm standing aside and watching the crosses.
