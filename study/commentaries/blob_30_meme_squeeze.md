What a fortnight. GameStop — a struggling mall retailer — traded above $450 today, up from under twenty dollars at the start of January, and the frenzy has spread to AMC, Bed Bath, BlackBerry, and whatever else the online boards decide to target. These are not fundamental buyers; they are a loosely coordinated army of retail accounts hunting the year's most crowded shorts, and the mechanics are brutal: short interest far above the float means covering begets covering. Melvin Capital has reportedly thrown in the towel at a devastating loss after an emergency infusion, and there are whispers of other funds in genuine distress. Brokerage platforms are now restricting trades in the hottest names, which has the crowd crying foul. Nobody I speak to can price this, and anyone short is terrified. This is not investing; it is a short squeeze elevated to a spectator sport.
