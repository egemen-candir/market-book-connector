I have to keep pinching myself at how far we've come since that Christmas Eve capitulation. The S&P is back near 2,950, almost fully retracing the fourth-quarter washout, and what strikes me most is the violence of the rotation back into anything with duration risk. Powell's patient pivot in January did the heavy lifting, but the credit market's message has been just as loud — investment-grade and high-yield new-issue windows swung wide open within weeks, spreads have compressed relentlessly, and leveraged loan supply is being absorbed without a whimper. Earnings season is wrapping up with modest beats, hardly spectacular, yet buyers meet every dip. Vol is pinned, the VIX flirts with the low teens, and the bears who swore the December lows would break are quietly covering. This isn't euphoria; it's relief compounded by a Fed that has stopped kicking the chair out.
