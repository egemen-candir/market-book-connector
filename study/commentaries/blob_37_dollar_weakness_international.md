The dollar is having its worst stretch in years — the index is down around ten percent since January, the worst first half I can remember in decades — and the usual playbook of hiding in US mega-caps while the currency does the work is simply not paying. For the first time in a long while, international equities are beating the S&P: European banks and defense names have ripped, the DAX is out front, and even Japanese stocks in dollar terms are keeping pace. I keep hearing it dismissed as a weak-dollar trade, but flows tell another story — global funds that have underweighted the rest of the world for a decade are quietly rebuilding non-US positions. Call it dollar weakness, call it valuation relief; either way, the crowd is on the wrong side of it.
