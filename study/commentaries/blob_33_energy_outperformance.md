Halfway through the year the scoreboard is almost embarrassing: energy is the only group meaningfully higher while the broad index sits in a bear market. Every dip in the majors gets bought and the sell-side keeps chasing targets higher. The cash flows are real — producers are printing money, paying special dividends and buying back stock, and income funds who ignored the sector for a decade are dragged back in. Meanwhile everything else fights margin compression, multiple deflation, and guidance cuts. My career was formed when energy was a value trap, and this reversal is striking: the market has decided hard assets and pricing power beat long-duration stories while inflation runs hot. The trade is getting crowded; a recession scare could still drag the laggard into the broad selloff. But managers who swore never again to own oil stocks are quietly rebuilding. That tells me the leadership is not finished.
