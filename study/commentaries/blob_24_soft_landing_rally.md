Halfway through 2023 and the soft-landing trade has become consensus almost without anyone noticing the handoff. June CPI printed at three percent, inflation is clearly rolling over, and the Fed still hiked last week to five-and-a-quarter — likely the last one, everyone now says. The S&P closed out July around 4,590, the best first half since 2019, yet the leadership is startlingly narrow: Nvidia's blowout guidance lit the AI fuse, and Microsoft, Apple, Alphabet, Amazon, Meta, and Tesla have done the real lifting while the equal-weight index badly lags. Advance-decline lines are weak, most stocks never participated, and strategists keep abandoning recession calls — jobless claims won't cooperate, banks passed their stress tests, and consumers keep spending. Ten-year Treasury is near four percent, credit spreads have tightened all year. I'm suspicious of a market where a handful of stocks are the trade, but fighting the tape has been expensive.
